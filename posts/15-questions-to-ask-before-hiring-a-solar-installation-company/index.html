Navigating the renewable energy market requires diligence. When evaluating a solar company, the difference between a profitable investment and a financial burden often lies in the contract details before you sign. This guide provides the definitive questions to ask solar installers to ensure you are choosing a partner that prioritizes safety, performance, and transparency.

The Importance of a Solar Installer Checklist

Before engaging with any provider, it is critical to establish a baseline for comparison. An effective solar company checklist prevents disputes over system design, equipment quality, and maintenance responsibilities later on. In the current market, where financing options vary widely, due diligence protects your equity in the home.

The following 15 questions are categorized into five core pillars: Credentials, System Design, Financials, Warranties, and Support. Use this framework to vet any choosing a solar installer candidate effectively.

1. Licensing and Qualifications

A reputable provider must be legally authorized to operate in your jurisdiction. Unlicensed contractors often void insurance policies and local permits.

What specific licenses does the company hold?

In most regions, electrical work requires a specialized license. Ask for proof of their contractor’s license number and verify it with your state or local licensing board. A valid license ensures they adhere to building codes.

Is the installer insured?

Request a certificate of insurance (COI). You need coverage for property damage during installation and liability if an injury occurs on-site. Uninsured installers pose significant financial risk to you if accidents happen.

Are there local certifications?

Look for certifications from the North American Board of Certified Energy Practitioners (NABCEP) or equivalent local bodies. These indicate that the team understands regional weather patterns, wind loads, and mounting requirements specific to your area.

2. System Design and Technology

The hardware you install dictates the longevity and efficiency of your energy production. You must understand exactly what is being placed on your roof.

Which equipment brands do you use?

Ask for the Tier status of their panels. Tier 1 manufacturers (e.g., Qcells, Longi, REC) generally offer better degradation rates than generic brands. Avoid installers who push obscure brands solely to save costs.

What inverters are recommended?

The inverter converts DC power from panels to AC power for your home. Ask if they use string inverters or microinverters. Microinverters often optimize performance on shaded roofs but cost more upfront. Ensure their choice matches your roof layout.

How does the system design account for shading?

A professional solar company uses tools like Helioscope or Aurora to model shadow loss from nearby trees or chimneys. Ask to see a 3D rendering of the proposed array. If they do not provide this, their generation estimates may be inaccurate.

What is the expected system lifespan?

High-quality panels typically last 25 to 30 years. Inverters often require replacement after 10 to 15 years. Ensure the proposal outlines the lifecycle of every component.

3. Generation Estimates and Performance

Verifying energy output prevents under-sizing your system or overpaying for capacity you do not need.

How are generation estimates calculated?

Estimates should be based on historical weather data for your specific zip code, not just national averages. Ask if they account for seasonal variations, snow load, and local microclimates.

Does the proposal include battery storage?

If considering storage, ask about compatibility with your home energy management system (HEMS). Ensure the installer can integrate the panels with a backup battery seamlessly.

What is the projected ROI timeline?

A standard solar company checklist includes a payback period calculation. Ask them to break down fixed costs versus variable savings to see if the timeline aligns with your financial goals.

Hidden fees are the most common complaint among homeowners who switch installers. Clarity in payment terms protects your budget.
